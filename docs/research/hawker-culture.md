# Hawker culture research for Prata Raja

Scope: skills, dips, places, music, lines, enemies. Sources marked (snippet) were seen in search results but not opened; treat as leads only.

## 1. Prata and roti craft
- Types (Kosong = plain; Telur = egg; Bawang = onion; Tisu = tissue-thin, sugar and condensed milk; Murtabak = thick, egg/meat/onion filling; Tampal/plaster = egg spread on one side; coin prata = small and crispy; Boom/bom = small, thick, spiral-wound; Planta = margarine and sugar; Sardin = canned sardine): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai
- Dough: flour, water and ghee or oil, kneaded, oiled, folded into layers, then tossed thin on the counter, gathered into a rope and wound into a spiral: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai
- Stretch must form a "windowpane" without tearing (cook's blog, snippet): https://sgpnoodles.substack.com/p/roti-prata
- Cooked on a tawa with plenty of oil: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai
- Hawker culture: many hawkers refine one dish for years and pass skills to family or apprentices: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/hawker-culture-in-singapore-community-dining-and-culinary-practices-in-a-multicultural-urban-context-01568
- No named flipping move found in sources. Invent names freely, flagged as game terms.

**Use in game:** Roti types map to the tags in docs/BUILDS.md (Tisu = Pierce/Crisp, Tampal = Stuffed, Murtabak = Area/Stuffed, Boom = Bounce). Skill names can use the real types. Stretch/toss moves are invented. Sounds: the dough slap on the tawa, oil sizzle, a crisp "flip" at the peak of the toss. Stall-counter lines: "Roti kosong satu, teh tarik kurang manis" (player's own line; fits how orders are spoken).

## 2. Dips, sides and drinks
- Roti canai is traditionally served with dhal curry; also fish curry and sambal (as sides): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai (dhal), sardine curry (snippet): https://www.reddit.com/r/MalaysianFood/comments/16qu59m/ikan_sardin_whats_your_favourite_way_of_eating/
- Teh tarik: "pulled tea", strong tea with condensed milk, poured between two vessels from height to froth it. Traditional with prata in Singapore: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik
- Ordering terms: kosong = no sugar; kurang manis = less sweet; Teh-C = evaporated milk; Teh-O = no milk; ais = iced: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik
- Kopi terms: Kopi-O = black with sugar; Kopi-C = evaporated milk, separate sugar; siew dai = less sweet; gao = thick; po = diluted: https://skymedia.com.sg/kopitiam-ordering-guide-for-mandarin-speakers/
- Teh halia = tea with ginger, warming: https://skymedia.com.sg/kopitiam-drinks-beyond-kopi-singapore/ (snippet)
- Milo dinosaur = iced Milo with undissolved Milo powder heaped on top: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo_dinosaur

**Use in game:** Teh tarik = the pull (long poured stream, as planned in docs/WORLD.md). Sambal = tears (blinds pests). Curry = slow burn. Dhal = comfort. Teh halia = warm, with a ginger heat. Milo dinosaur = the shield drink from docs/BUILDS.md. Kosong/siew dai/po can be order-tag lines for regulars.

## 3. Places and set dressing
- Kopitiam: communal seating; strangers share tables: https://eatdrinktravel.com/singapore-hawker-centres/
- Chope: a packet of tissue on an occupied-looking table reserves it. A table with personal items but no people is claimed; an empty table is fair game at peak hours: https://skymedia.com.sg/kopitiam-ordering-guide-for-mandarin-speakers/ and https://eatdrinktravel.com/singapore-hawker-centres/
- Tray return: return trays at marked stations. Cleaners clear tables, but leaving your own tray is poor etiquette: https://eatdrinktravel.com/singapore-hawker-centres/ and NParks cites tray-return practices in its myna section: https://avs.nparks.gov.sg/wildlife/encountering-wildlife/house-crows-pigeons-javan-mynas/javan-mynas/
- Hawker centres as "community dining rooms" (breakfast to dinner, chess, busking): https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/hawker-culture-in-singapore-community-dining-and-culinary-practices-in-a-multicultural-urban-context-01568
- UNESCO inscription in 2020 (Hawker culture, first Singapore inscription): https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/hawker-culture-in-singapore-community-dining-and-culinary-practices-in-a-multicultural-urban-context-01568
- Pasar malam = "night market", evening street market in residential areas, brightly lit stalls, games and rides, runs a few days before festivals, location may change: https://bestinsingapore.co/guide-to-pasar-malam/
- Tekka Centre (Little India): wet market, hawker centre and shops in one building, opened 1982, 665 Buffalo Road; fresh seafood (Sri Lankan crabs), vegetables, Chinese stalls selling imported vegetables; Chinese stallholders often speak Tamil: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekka_Centre
- Geylang Serai: Ramadan and Hari Raya bazaar around Wisma Geylang Serai, 500+ stalls, Ramly burgers, vadai, biryani, baju kurung and baju melayu: https://www.littledayout.com/geylang-serai-hari-raya-ramadan-bazaar-guide/
- Deepavali: illuminated arches and lamps on Serangoon Road; kolam on shopfronts; clay diyas; rangoli powder and stencils at the bazaar: https://www.singapore-spirit.com/guides/deepavali-singapore/

**Use in game:** Chapter 1 counter: chope tissue packets, tray-return bins, a regular's "claimed" table. Chapter 2: hawker centre at night, neon, stall lamps. Chapter 3 pasar malam: lamp strings, rides as a backdrop, pop-up stalls with a fresh swap each run. Chapter 4 Tekka: crates of crabs, tiled walls, spice sacks. Festival set dressing (Deepavali lights, Geylang Serai lanterns) only as background, never a target.

## 4. Music and visual motifs (Tamil)
- Nadaswaram: auspicious wind instrument, played in pairs with two thavil drums, at Hindu weddings and temples; very loud: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadaswaram
- Urumi: double-headed hourglass drum, played with sticks while walking; pitch bends from squeezing the rope: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urumi_(drum). Urumi melam is tied to funerals and inauspicious events, so avoid it for victory or fanfare sounds.
- Mridangam: Carnatic percussion, double-headed, jackfruit-wood body, tuned by leather laces. Children's mridangamela in temple festivals: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mridangam
- Kolam: rice-flour designs drawn at dawn on thresholds; offered to deities; kept sacred and not stepped on: http://www.sahapedia.org/significance-of-kolam-tamil-culture
- Jasmine garlands: used in weddings and festivals (snippet): https://www.hithesh-flowers.com/jasmine-garlands-weddings/
- Thaipusam route music: musicians line the 3.2 km route, roughly 7am to 10pm: https://www.sassymamasg.com/guide-to-thaipusam-singapore/

**Use in game:** BGM: a light nadaswaram-and-thavil pulse for the stall's hub and boss intros. Use a mridangam loop for pasar malam. UI frames: kolam line-art borders on the menu board and card frames (brass kolam is already in docs/WORLD.md). Effects: jasmine petal sparkles on crit. Urumi-style rope squeeze as a "talking drum" pitch slide on pest-spawn stings.

## 5. Singlish lines (light, short)
Definitions from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singlish_vocabulary unless marked.
1. "Eh, first one burn? Just ask again lor." (lor = obvious, resigned; already in milestones.ts)
2. "Walao, your prata so tall!" (walao = exclamation of shock)
3. "Sia, that bread got fire." (sia = exclamation)
4. "Chope this table lah, I come back." (chope = reserve a seat)
5. "Can, one more kosong." (can = yes to a request)
6. "Don't kiasu lah, eat slowly." (kiasu = afraid of losing)
7. "Shiok leh!" (shiok = delight at good food; leh = emphasis)
8. "Aiyo, the mynah stole it!" (aiyo = oh dear)
9. "Lepak here later, after the rush." (lepak = relax)
10. "Makan first, fight later." (makan = eat)
11. "Kopi O kosong, no sugar, and bring the tray back." (kosong = zero; tray etiquette)
12. "Siew dai, less sweet, ok?" (siew dai = less sweet, from skymedia kopi guide)
13. "Your tawa hot like that, cannot fail hygiene one." (playful, for the Grade Inspector)
14. "Don't scold me, I just fry bread." (light, not mean)
15. "Eh, that rat very bold, like got tenancy." (joke, no group)
16. "Quick lah, the rush is coming." (plain English with lah; no source needed)
17. "Ai yah, don't waste the curry." (ai yah = mild regret)
18. "Ok lah, one more round then sleep." (ok lah = accept)
19. "Good prata, uncle will come back tomorrow, confirm." (confirm = certain, neutral)
20. "Raja! Raja! Two kosong, one tissue!" (already in milestones.ts; cheering crowd)
Avoid: dialect-ethnic punchlines, ethnic accents, and any line that mocks a faith.

**Use in game:** regulars' lines (uncles, aunties), milestone one-liners, find-pop captions. Keep to one line per bubble, short enough for phones.

## 6. Pests and bosses
- Javan mynas: introduced in the 1920s through the caged-bird trade; now very common; roost in large groups; NParks uses spikes on perching spots in hawker centres and food-waste control: https://avs.nparks.gov.sg/wildlife/encountering-wildlife/house-crows-pigeons-javan-mynas/javan-mynas/
- Monitor lizards: Malayan water monitor is the common one; shy and avoid people; food waste draws them: https://avs.nparks.gov.sg/wildlife/encountering-wildlife/monitor-lizards/. Keep as a "stays in the drain" hazard, not a combat boss.
- Hygiene grading: NEA's old A/B/C/D grade was a single annual snapshot; the newer Bronze/Silver/Gold award tracks consistency over time, and demerit points still count: https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/readers-letters/index/new-grading-system-takes-into-account-the-track-record-of-eateries-from-every-hygiene-inspection
- Cockroach infestation counts as a major demerit (snippet): https://www.todayonline.com/singapore/nea-introduce-new-food-hygiene-recognition-scheme-2020
- Crows, pigeons, rats: no opened source yet. Snippet lead: https://mothership.sg/2022/12/hawker-centre-birds-book/

**Use in game:** Mynahs = steal-and-flee (as in WORLD.md), spiked perches as a visual. Rats pop from longkang grates. The Grade Inspector boss can carry clipboard, sign and stickers. Keep it a spirit with no real officer, no real agency logo, no branding. A grade sticker is a milestone gift.

## Sensitivities
- Thaipusam: sacred; devotees pierce skin with skewers and carry kavadi. No mocking, no using it as a hazard, no skewer-shot gags. Spectators are asked to stay respectful (sassymamasg source above).
- Kolam: treated as sacred; do not "stomp" or "destroy" it as a gameplay event. Use it as decoration only (sahapedia source).
- Urumi melam: linked to funerals; avoid as victory fanfare.
- Temples and deities: no religious figures as enemies or jokes, including Ganesha, Lakshmi and Murugan.
- Ramadan and Hari Raya: no fasting or prayer jokes; treat the bazaar as festive background only.
- NEA and Grade Inspector: comic spirit only, no real officer, no real logo or wording claiming official authority.
- Ethnicity: avoid dialect accents, stereotyped "uncle" or "aunty" caricatures, and pejorative slang. Keep Singlish lines short and affectionate.
- Mynahs and monitor lizards: protected or wild animals. Keep them in-game only; no real-world advice to harm them.
- Sources: several links are travel blogs and snippets. Verify before final copy, and prefer NEA, NParks, UNESCO and Wikipedia.
